The program — hot path and the dance

Read from the code on 2026-08-30. What an hour of radio is supposed to sound like, what the maker does to get there, and what the player does to put it on air. Companion to handoff.html, which covers the live station; this covers app/(program)/.

In one breath

A request ("Saturday night 80s, Dallas, hits-forward") becomes a pre-generated hour: Claude picks the records, writes a card for each, lays out the clock, writes every word; ElevenLabs voices it with character timestamps; a pure function turns all of that into an ordered Element[] the player can run. The player is a reducer with three lanes — the Spotify device, the voice, a looped bed — and one effects hook that makes the lanes real. Every timing is derived (a clip's length, a card's intro, a known character offset in the alignment); nothing is guessed at runtime, and every place the good version couldn't be had takes the next rung of a ladder and says so.

The whole design in one rule. Optimistic and fail-safe: do the cool thing when the numbers support it (land the post, talk the song in under the bed's fade, dry legal ID then bed), otherwise start with the song. Nothing ever stalls the show — a failed clip is CLIP_FAILED, which is treated exactly like CLIP_ENDED.

1. The hot path — what should happen on air

The player reads one file, program.json, whose elements are the reducer's own type (reducer.ts):

type Element =
  | { kind: "song"; track: Track; talk?: { clip; over: "intro" | "outro"; atMs? } }
  | { kind: "break"; clip; bed?; bedInMs?; leadMs; label }

Four treatments the log can give a record (Intro in shapes.ts) become those two kinds:

TreatmentElements it becomesWhat you hear
breakbreak (bed) then songVoice over a bed; the last line "talks the next song in" — the song starts ducked under it, comes up full when the clip ends.
talkupsong with talk.over = "intro"The song starts full; the voice comes in atMs into the intro (ducking it) and ends a beat before the first sung word — or, when the card isn't sure, 1.5 s in.
sweeperbreak (no bed, leadMs = 0) then songA short dry ID, then a hard intro.
seguesongStraight out of the last record.

A break, the ideal version

This is the one with the most moving parts. Take the real opening from the last walk: a 40.6 s clip, leadMs = 6848, no legal ID (not the top of the hour), so the bed comes in at 0.

Bed up over 400 ms from bedInMs; fades over BED_FADE_MS (1500) so it reaches zero exactly at clip − leadMs. The next song is started PREROLL_MS (350) before that moment at the duck level (0.3); when the clip ends, CLIP_ENDED ramps it to full (0.8) over 500 ms in 25 steps. full ducked voice bed

Three things had to line up for that picture: the script wrote a separate leadLine; ElevenLabs returned an alignment; timingsOf() found the lead line's first character at a sane offset. Any one missing → lead → end: leadMs = 0, the song starts when the clip ends (still fine, just a hard intro).

A talk-up, the ideal version

music

0atMs− 400 ms beatpost (introMs)
atMs = introMs − clipMs − BEAT_MS, only when card.sure and that number is ≥ 0. The device plays the song full from 0; a timer fires TALK_DUE at atMs, the reducer ducks the music and puts the clip on the mic; CLIP_ENDED brings the music back to full.

In the last walk every card came back sure: false (Opus is honest about "within a second or two"), so every talk-up took post → late: same shape, but atMs = TALKUP_LATE_MS (1500) — the song is clearly under way, then the voice comes in, and a long intro (24–38 s on those records) absorbs it. The log still only requires introMs ≥ 7000; sure decides between the two rungs in assembly. There is no talk-up at all if the clip failed: late → none, a plain song.

The top of the hour

The clock starts at PROGRAM_START_MS (8:43 pm). hourAtSeqOf() walks the log — each break costs BREAK_MS (30 s) plus each record's length — and the first slot that starts past 9:00 is the top of the hour: forced to break with topOfHour = true, whatever the model said. Its script line gets a separate legalId; it is spoken dry and the bed comes in at the character right after it (bedInMs). No alignment there → bedIn → start: the bed simply runs from 0. If an earlier break is closer than 3 songs, it is the one demoted to a sweeper ("2 songs before the top of the hour"), never the legal ID.

2. The maker's dance (program side)

Everything under apps/web/src/app/(program)/program/make/. Five stateless stages, each a POST /program/make/<stage> that reads the previous stage's file and writes its own under apps/web/public/program/make/ (gitignored, hand-editable, served by Next as static files). Dev only — production answers 404, because they write into the app's own tree. /program/make is a desk that runs them: all (Make), one (Run), or "from here →".

Five stages, five files

discover
reads: body
writes: request.json, picks.json
1 Opus call (effort high) → N picks; each resolved by Spotify search, first hit; dedupe; ≥ 6 records or 422
enrich
reads: picks.json, cards/*
writes: cards/<id>.json, picks.json (dropped)
1 Opus call per record (medium), 5 at a time; a valid card on disk is reused (0 tokens); refusal retried once, then the record is dropped
log
reads: request, picks, cards
writes: log.json
1 Opus call (high) → order + treatment per record; then checkLog() enforces the clock
script
reads: request, picks, cards, log
writes: script.json
1 Opus call (high) → every word; legalId and leadLine are separate fields, kept only where they apply
voice
reads: all of the above
writes: clips/slot-<seq>.mp3, program.json
ElevenLabs with-timestamps, 2 at a time; timings at known offsets; assemble() → Element[] + notes

First walk, 12 records: 24.6 s · 50.3 s · 14 s · 19 s · 33 s ≈ 2.4 min. Re-running enrich reused all cards in 29 ms.

The contract between stages is zod (shapes.ts). readJson() answers 409 missing: log.json when the previous stage hasn't run and 400 log.json: slots[0].intro — Invalid option… when you hand-edited it wrong — and writes nothing. That is the whole "iterate on a file, run from that stage" loop: log.json is the one you'll edit most (change a talkup to a segue, reorder), then Run script and voice.

How Claude is asked

ask.ts: one shape for all four calls — SYSTEM (a CHR/Top 40 PD, 80s–90s style) + a brief, a single forced strict tool (tool_choice: {type: "tool"}, additionalProperties: false, every property required so "" / 0 means "none"), adaptive thinking, output_config.effort per stage, max_tokens 16000, model from env().CLAUDE_MODEL. A stop_reason === "refusal" is a MakeError 502 ("refused"). Prompts live in prompts.ts, not in the settings table — this is the sandbox, they iterate in code. The enrich tool puts a free-text thinking property first on purpose: the model reasons about the record's intro before it commits to introMs/sure.

What each brief carries: discover gets the station line and the request and is told this is its room to be creative; enrich gets one record (artist, title, album, the resolved version's length); log gets every record with its card, RULES_TEXT verbatim, and how many minutes to the hour; script gets the log too and is told the lead line is a separate field never repeated in words.

checkLog — the hard rules (pure, tested)

clock-rules.ts holds the constants, the same rules in prose (RULES_TEXT, inlined into the log brief so prompt and validator can't drift), and the check. Order of operations, because it matters:

  1. Unknown ids dropped, duplicates dropped, unplaced records appended as segues (warnings, not fallbacks).
  2. Slot 0 must be a break — segue → break "the first slot is the opening".
  3. A talkup needs a card with introMs ≥ 7000 — else talkup → segue. (sure is not checked here; see the hot path.)
  4. The hour: the slot where 9:00 turns is promoted to the top-of-the-hour break; any other topOfHour is cleared with a warning.
  5. Spacing: breaks closer than 3 songs — the later one becomes a sweeper, unless the later one is the legal ID, in which case the earlier (non-opening) one gives way. Farther than 4 is only a warning.

Every downgrade lands in log.json.fallbacks with its reason; the /program/make row summarises "12 slots: 3 break, 3 talkup … · 2 fallbacks".

Timings from alignment, never search

The text sent to ElevenLabs is exactly [legalId, words, leadLine].join(" ") (textOf()), so two offsets are known before the audio exists:

bedInMs = start of character  legalId.length + 1            // the space after the ID
leadAt  = start of character  text.length − leadLine.length // where the lead line begins
leadMs  = clipMs − leadAt
clipMs  = the last character's end time

timingsOf() only accepts an offset whose start time is monotonic with its neighbour and inside the clip; anything odd is left out and assembly takes the rung below. Nothing is ever searched for in the text or the audio — that is the "optimistic, fail-safe" ask made concrete.

assemble — the ladder

assemble.ts is pure: log + script + measured clips + the produced-sweeper list → Program. Each element that carries a clip gets a Note ({element, seq, treatment, words, clip, clipMs?, bedInMs?, leadMs?, atMs?, fallback?}); the player shows them. The full ladder:

Because the ladder always bottoms out at "a song", voice writes program.json even with no ELEVENLABS_KEY — every clip rejected, every treatment on its last rung, still playable.

3. The player's dance (UI/UX side)

/program (program.tsx) fetches program.json, validates it with the Program zod shape, and hands elements to useProgram(). The Spotify account is this browser's (same PKCE flow and cookie identity as the station's home page); the tab becomes the device through useSpotifyDevice.

Three lanes, one reducer

reducer.ts is pure and says what each lane should be doing; it never touches audio:

music: { uri, level: off | duck | full }   // the Spotify device — songs only
mic:   clip name | null                     // the voice, an <audio> element
bed:   bed name | null                      // a looped instrumental under the voice
playSeq / micSeq                            // bump = that lane must (re)start
cursor, loop, startedAt, error

lanesFor(el) is the whole "what does an element start with" table: a break → music off, mic = clip, bed = its bed; a song with an intro talk and no atMs → music ducked + mic; every other song → music full, mic silent. The two worlds are deliberately separate: the device's audio is DRM'd and can't enter Web Audio, so its only knob is setVolume stepped from a timer; the mic and the bed are ours, mixed in one AudioContext, with the bed a single looping buffer that starts at mount and only ever has its gain moved — scheduled on the audio clock, so it lands exactly and never clicks.

The tap: unlock, connect, run

Browsers only let audio start inside a user gesture, and the device may not exist yet. go(event) in program.tsx does everything that must happen synchronously in the tap, then defers the rest:

  1. unlock() — resume the AudioContext, and play a few ms of WAV silence through the mic <audio> so iOS lets later play()s through.
  2. device.activate() — the SDK's own gesture requirement.
  3. If the device is ready, dispatch (RUN / JUMP) now; otherwise remember the event in armed, call device.connect(), and an effect dispatches it when status turns ready (or drops it on error).

Run is disabled until identity says product === "premium" and the stored account is loaded. RUN starts at the cursor (or 0) and stamps startedAt, which the on-screen clock reads from PROGRAM_START_MS. Stop silences all lanes but keeps the cursor.

The seven effects (use-program.ts)

#WatchesDoes
1elements (mount)Fetches every clip → object URL, measures its length with a throw-away <audio> (Chrome reports a streamed mp3 through durationchange); fetches and decodes the bed, starts it looping at gain 0. The whole program is known up front.
2playSeq, musicUriA new element: setVolume(level) then device.play([uri], 0); a rejection is HALT. A separate effect on level ducks instantly (down) or ramps up over 500 ms / 25 steps (the SDK has no fade).
3the break on air + mic positionarmBed(posMs, durMs): from wherever the voice is in its clip, schedule the bed's gain — up to 0.12 (≈ −18 dB) over 400 ms at bedInMs, flat, then down to 0 over 1500 ms landing at clip − leadMs. Re-armed on play and on seek.
4mic, micSeqTap the <audio> into the graph once, set src, play(); onended → CLIP_ENDED, onerror/rejected play → CLIP_FAILED; reports a micClock every 250 ms. Keyed on micSeq, not playSeq, so a lead can restart the music lane while the clip keeps talking.
5a waiting talk + device playbackArms TALK_DUE: an intro talk at atMs − pos, an outro talk at duration − clipLen − 1000 − pos. Re-armed on every playback report (a seek moves the due time). More than 1 s overdue → skipped, never repeated.
6a break with a lead + micClockArms LEAD_DUE at clipLen − leadMs − 350 − pos: the song is called 350 ms early so it sounds where the bed's fade lands.
7runningStopped → pause everything, bed off, device paused; unmount → close the graph.

Who fires what, and what the reducer does

EventFromReducer
CLIP_ENDED / CLIP_FAILEDthe mic elementOn a break: next element. On a song's intro talk (its own, or a break's lead that carried over): mic off, bed off, music → full. On an outro talk: mic off, wait for the track to end.
TRACK_ENDEDuseSpotifyDevice.onTrackListEndedNext element.
TALK_DUEeffect 5Mic = the talk's clip, music → duck.
LEAD_DUEeffect 6Cursor moves to the next song, music = that uri, ducked, playSeq++ — micSeq untouched, so the break's clip (and its fading bed) keep going.
HALTplay() refused, device lostStop with an error line on the desk.
JUMP / NEXT / PREVthe cue sheet, transportmoveTo(index): lanes from lanesFor, both seqs bumped.

The subtle one is the lead hand-off: for ~7 s the cursor is on the song while the mic is still playing the break's clip. CLIP_ENDED then finds a song on air whose mic matches → "the clip was the song's intro", music comes back up. That is why the reducer checks s.mic === e.clip rather than the element's own clip.

The timeline (cue sheet)

timeline.tsx: one row per element, two lane bars. A song's axis is the track (music bar = its level; mic bar = the clip's measured length, pushed to the end for an outro). A break's axis is its clip (the "music" bar shows the bed from bedInMs to the lead). A white hairline follows the device's reported position or the micClock. The title jumps there; a click on a bar scrubs — on the row on air it seeks at once (device.seek or seekMic, the bed following via onseeked); on another row it jumps first and the seek lands once that lane reports playing. Under the title, the maker's note: the treatment, an amber from → to badge with the reason on hover, and the words in a <details>.

6. What's open

  • Not verified live: Run on /program with a Premium account after the maker (the page loads and validates program.json; the audio dance above is what the code does — listen for the lead hand-off and the bed fade). The bad-ELEVENLABS_KEY run is covered by tests only.
  • Discovery takes the first search hit — two album/remaster versions crept in (6:14, 5:44). Preferring the shortest hit is an easy follow-up in discover.ts's resolve().
  • Landing the post on unsure cards when the margin is wide (e.g. a 30 s intro and a 12 s clip): today sure: false always means the late rung. A rule like "land it if introMs − clipMs − BEAT ≥ some slack" belongs in assemble.ts.
  • Outro talks exist in the reducer and the player (over: "outro", back-timed with TAIL_MS) but the maker never writes one — the card's outro/outroMs are collected and unused.
  • Produced sweepers are optional; with none, a sweeper slot without words is a segue.
  • Prompts are in code, not in settings; no provenance of which prompt made a program beyond card.model and madeAt.

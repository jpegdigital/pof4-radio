Coverage is intentionally limited by usable excerpts: Google discovery does not yet resolve to permitted publisher evidence, and the roster has no direct world-only feed. Human review, licensed full-text providers, robust cross-publisher event identity and proactive correction monitoring remain further work. Source retention and commercial syndication permissions still need a provider-by-provider decision before broadening distribution.

Recommendation: keep request-driven production. Pull a curated mix of local reporting, official updates, music and culture, plus broad news discovery when an upcoming break needs it. Rank distinct stories, retrieve evidence for the shortlist, write a brief sourced sentence, and remember the version actually heard.

Use Google News as a discovery input during iteration. It should not be the sole editorial desk or the evidence behind a generated explanation. Start with plain fetch, the existing Postgres, and code beside the session routes. Add a worker only after measured demand warrants it.

There is no single canonical “2026 AI radio headline architecture.” The defensible foundation is editorial sourcing plus retrieval, revision tracking, freshness, and evaluation. The station-specific choices below are proposals, not industry standards. The product ambition is credible; a claim that nobody else has achieved it is not established by this research.

3. What good practice and SOTA mean here

Canonical foundation: sourced reporting, explicit uncertainty, corrections

AP's published guidance treats generative output as unvetted material and keeps editorial accountability with journalists. It does not endorse autonomous publication. Our adaptation is to constrain automation to inspectable evidence, allow omission, and provide an editorial review path for sensitive stories. A model saying it is confident is not verification. AP standards.

The BBC/EBU's October 2025 evaluation found significant issues in 45% of more than 3,000 tested assistant answers, with sourcing and accuracy among the problems. That is evidence for testing claims and attribution; it is not an estimated failure rate for our model or a forecast for 2026 systems. EBU study summary.

Strong contemporary pattern: trusted retrieval before generation

EBU Neo offers a useful deployed reference: trusted member content, explicit time constraints, keyword plus semantic retrieval, relevance grading/ranking, source links and user feedback. It is a news question-answering system, not proof of a successful music-radio editorial system. Adopt the evidence flow; do not import its whole infrastructure. EBU Neo technical account, September 2025.

Modern aggregation separates duplicate copies from related reporting. NewsCatcher documents both deduplication and embedding-based clustering. One wire story copied to twenty sites is one reporting origin, not twenty independent confirmations. Cluster by event and revision as well as topic: two unrelated incidents in Dallas must remain distinct. Deduplication; clustering.

Research also explores balancing personalization and diversity during retrieval itself. A 2025 VQ-VAE news recommendation study evaluated that trade-off on MIND. It supports keeping a broad menu available, but does not justify training a recommender for this station with no listener dataset. Begin with visible editorial dimensions and human evaluation. Democratizing News Recommenders.

The competitive bar is already above “AI reads local news”

Futuri AudioAI advertises real-time localized news, weather, events and trend-driven content. Its Audio Briefs product advertises source, vibe and local-news preferences. Super Hi-Fi and Trinity Audio announced automated audio news briefings in 2022. These are provider claims and product descriptions, not independent quality comparisons. AudioAI; Audio Briefs; Super Hi-Fi announcement.

Our differentiation hypothesis: local editorial taste + memory of what was actually heard + meaningful story developments + traceable facts + excellent transitions over the listener's own records. Prove that combination in listening trials. No exhaustive competitor audit or “first ever” claim is implied.

5. What earns airtime

Optimize for “glad I heard that.” Local usefulness, public importance, discovery and a concrete new development matter more than popularity or an easy pun into the next track. Musical fit decides delivery and placement after factual eligibility.

First gate: can we responsibly say it?

  1. Usable source and time: attributable reporting or first-party evidence, a valid publication/update time, a relevant place, and a still-valid event. Unknown date, future publication beyond a small clock-skew allowance, missing source or evidence failure means skip until resolved.
  2. Enough evidence for the sentence: title plus a permitted article excerpt or structured official record that supports every factual clause. A title can be a lead; it must not invite invented causes, consequences, numbers or certainty. If access fails, choose another story.
  3. Correct type and qualification: distinguish report, analysis, opinion, promotion, allegation and confirmed outcome. Preserve “proposed,” “expected,” “officials say” and disagreements when material.
  4. Risk-sensitive corroboration: routine venue notices can rely on the venue; contentious allegations, casualties and rapidly changing high-impact reports need independent evidence and editorial review or are omitted in the initial unattended mode. Two syndicated copies do not satisfy independence. This is a product policy proposal, not a universal two-source rule.
  5. Worth revisiting: new event or a material supported change since the last selected/heard version. Retitled copies and timestamp-only updates are not new developments.

Then rank distinct stories, not individual URLs

Normalize canonical URLs and provider IDs first. Group near-identical syndicated copies. Match remaining items by actors, action, location and event time; use a bounded model comparison for ambiguous pairs in the small shortlist. Keep a persistent story ID and explicit revision/claim hash. An embedding can propose a match; it must not merge unrelated events merely because both mention Dallas or Iran.

Score eligible stories on 0–5 rubrics, with one sentence explaining each judgment. This starting formula is deliberately easy to inspect:

score = 5 × local usefulness
      + 5 × public importance
      + 4 × meaningful new development
      + 3 × discovery / human interest
      + 2 × fit to this hour and listener's stated interests
      + 1 × ease of a clear spoken explanation
      − 4 × recent topic saturation

Eligibility, stale facts and repeated unchanged stories are gates.
Popularity is a discovery hint, not a score multiplier.

Cap explicit-interest influence so the station still surprises people and carries consequential civic news. Do not infer politics or other sensitive traits from music taste. Review subject and publisher diversity over the last hour; do not manufacture “balance” between supported facts and unsupported claims.

Default output: one story in 15–25 spoken seconds; two only if the combined news portion stays under 35 seconds and each earns its place. Aim for a substantive local/service/culture contribution in each listening hour when qualifying supply exists. Track the opportunity count so an empty supply is distinguishable from a bad selector. A break may contain no news and still have weather, music context and the legal ID.

Give the writer facts, not a pile of enticing titles

Use one compact evidence packet per eligible story: IDs, dates, place, source links, reporting origin, 1–3 supported claims with excerpt references, uncertainty, the changed fact, why it matters, expiry and a suggested attribution. Delimit external text as untrusted data. News selection must be structured; do not mine model thinking for the choice afterward.

Initial quality-first path: deterministic shortlist → evidence retrieval for at most six stories → one editor call choosing a story and drafting its news sentence → a separate evidence-check call over that sentence and the original excerpts. Mechanical checks enforce valid IDs, evidence references, dates, numbers and length; the evidence checker assesses unsupported implications and missing qualifications. Neither checker establishes real-world truth. A failed or ambiguous check drops the news; at most one bounded repair is allowed in the deadline.

Pass the approved newsWords to the music writer as a fixed insertion. Keep music lead-in and news words separate in the structured shape, then compose the break deterministically. Do not allow the final stylistic pass to quietly change factual wording. Keep source attribution audible for each story and a tappable source receipt in the rundown.

Illustrative only: with a supported venue change, the first break might say, “The venue says Friday's show is moving indoors; existing tickets still work.” A later break should speak again only if there is a real change: “An update on that show: doors are now at seven, according to the venue.”

The distinctive behavior is the supported change and the remembered context. Never invent a local connection, joke through casualties, or force a tragic story into a lyrical segue. When serious news warrants airtime, use a neutral transition and breathing room.

6. When to pull, refresh, cache and air

Four independent clocks: transport validation time, publisher publication/update time, event validity, and actual playback time. Fetching an old article now does not make the event new. A 304 validates an unchanged response; it does not renew a story's editorial age.

Initial tunable policy The times below are station defaults to evaluate. Provider cache directives, rate limits and permitted storage take precedence where applicable. Conditional requests and response freshness have a real standard; radio editorial lifetimes do not. RFC 9111. AP also documents conditional requests and continuous update delivery as separate access patterns. AP update guide.

Request path and bounded waiting

  1. Browser reaches an upcoming break under the existing one-ahead loop. Read-only precheck confirms the slot needs a write; load news settings and pull/refresh a shared per-source snapshot before acquiring the long session transaction.
  2. Acquire the existing session lock and re-read the slot and settings version. If another request finished it, return the kept slot. Reuse the completed snapshot; do not repeat the fetch under the lock. This may occasionally do an unnecessary read but avoids holding a database lock through source discovery.
  3. Under the existing production ownership, load session news history, shortlist, build evidence, edit/check, write and voice. Keep initial coordination readable. Log transaction duration; do not introduce leases simply to make a diagram prettier.
  4. Start with a 6 s total discovery deadline, 4 s per endpoint, 6 s evidence phase with at most six article requests, and 20 s total news-edit/check deadline. These add latency to a cold first break; benchmark them. Abort outstanding work at each deadline. A timeout omits news and lets ordinary slot production continue.

A module-local Map plus one in-flight promise per source is enough for the first single-process trial. Use Promise.allSettled for independent sources and bounded concurrency, clear the in-flight entry in finally, and timestamp successful completion. Store a snapshot receipt for every produced news decision. Add durable per-source refresh state in Postgres when reloads/replicas or experiment comparison make it useful; Redis is not needed.

The long-pause problem must be explicit

The current break is one combined audio clip. A player cannot remove just a stale news sentence from it. Therefore introduce a deliberate live-vs-replay contract, not a silent change to again:

  • Live continuation: before a news-bearing cue first starts or resumes after its validity window, run a bounded server preflight. If still current, play. If expired or corrected, create a new news revision and clip for the unplayed break, or a news-free break preserving the legal ID and music lead-in. Keep the original artifact and receipt.
  • While refresh is pending: keep playing the current track/bed where possible; never stall indefinitely. If no safe clip is ready within the playback budget, use the defined music fallback and carry any still-due legal ID to a fresh voice element. Implement and test that fallback rather than assuming an old monolithic clip can be edited in place.
  • Archive replay: intentionally play the saved show, visibly dated as recorded; no promise of current news. Replay does not create a new live exposure or refresh facts. Corrections remain visible alongside the affected take.
  • Already heard: do not rewrite history. Flag the old statement, prevent further live use, and issue a sourced correction at the next eligible break if the listener heard the affected claim.

Before the preflight/revision path ships, restrict the pilot to active live sessions and omit volatile stories. Do not claim the stale-playback problem is solved by shortening the feed TTL.

Start with two headline tables: headline_snapshot (bounded discovery/evidence receipts and per-source health) and headline_story (stable identity, revision and current claims). Add news JSONB to session_slot for the selected/omitted decision. Add a small append-only session_news_exposure table when playback acknowledgment lands, unique by session/slot/take/story/revision. Use plain SQL at the route, constraints and indexes for the real queries, and db/schema/*.sql rather than migration files.

Keep revision records append-only within the snapshot/receipt; do not overwrite the evidence used by an old clip. Keep source metadata, hashes and our decision receipts according to the station policy; raw publisher text and permanent spoken derivatives must follow the enabled source's retention/use policy. “Everything kept forever” needs this explicit source-content exception.

Exposure means the news audio interval actually started/completed, not that a slot was generated or its track loaded. To measure it, voice news as a separately addressable part or capture a reliable news interval during composition. Initial suppression can use selection reservations conservatively, but must not say “as you heard” until playback acknowledgment exists. A lost browser receipt means unknown, not definitely heard. Session-local history is the default; cross-session listener memory needs an explicit identity design, since this app has no user table.

Implementation boundaries and failure policy

Use a maintained XML parser if expanding to heterogeneous RSS/Atom; the reproduced CDATA/entity defects justify evaluating one focused dependency. First check the lockfile and required security/options. If remaining Google-only temporarily, implement the narrow fixes with fixtures. Do not grow a home-made general XML parser to avoid one justified dependency.

Evidence fetches use configured HTTPS origins, bounded redirects with validation at every hop, blocked private/link-local destinations, body limits and timeouts. Never fetch arbitrary URLs supplied by article prose or the model. Extract text only, strip active markup, and give the editor no tools or secrets. Render titles/excerpts as text in the receipt view. Treat prompt-injection strings in feeds as data to be ignored and test that behavior.

Keep authentication consistent with the control room for tuning/refresh endpoints; the ordinary session request may only use the configured roster. Since dev and production share the database, db:plan is a review step before any later db:apply; isolate experiment rows by explicit run IDs and never clear shared sessions as part of headline testing.

Rollback: a settings value disables news while preserving music/weather/ID production. For old slots lacking receipts, treat freshness as unknown in live mode and use recorded replay semantics or rebuild a safe break. Do not revert to unsourced title expansion as the failure fallback. Any new fields must tolerate legacy rows during deployment.

Complexity trade-off: the extra editor and checker calls are justified by factual quality and source receipts; their value must be measured. A module cache is deliberately process-local; cross-replica coordination is deferred. Named policy values live in settings or a colocated configuration object during the CLI pilot. No speculative abstraction exceptions are otherwise needed.

8. Prove that it is better

Start with 120 saved break opportunities spanning at least seven days and multiple dayparts: quiet news, heavy news, DFW service changes, music releases, duplicate wires, resurfaced old stories, disputes, corrections and partial outages. Use identical snapshots, listener requests and upcoming tracks for the baseline and proposed selector. Hold out a subset from tuning; keep live trials separate so changing news cannot masquerade as a better prompt.

Tests that earn their keep

Follow the repository's test-first convention for pure nontrivial logic. Table-test malformed entities/CDATA, missing dates, timezone boundaries, canonicalization, same-event matching, syndicated origins, eligibility, changed claims, topic saturation and decision composition. Test cache boundaries with injected clocks/fetch: exact TTL, 304, timeout, mixed success, empty success, Retry-After, concurrent misses, rejection cleanup and changed source configuration.

Verify route/database and actual provider behavior in an isolated live run, without external APIs in CI. Add end-to-end checks for prepare → TTS failure → retry → play, pause past expiry, skip the news interval, correction after playback and archive replay. Run pnpm check and pnpm --filter web build when implementation changes ship; this documentation-only audit ran the two relevant existing test files and the live smoke instead.

Use a simple operator report before building dashboards: source health, candidate/story counts, article-access failures, rejected reasons, selected score reasons, stale fallbacks, omitted-news rate, repeat rate and live freshness at air. Keep evidence for editorial decisions rather than treating a successful HTTP response or model confidence as a quality metric.

9. When a worker becomes worthwhile

Stay live and request-driven while learning the editorial shape. No listeners means no fetching. A durable snapshot can improve restarts and multiple requests without a worker; a worker is a scheduling decision, not a prerequisite for high-quality headlines.

Reconsider after a week of measurements if cold preparation repeatedly misses the news deadline, multiple active processes duplicate substantial paid retrieval, per-market refresh needs outlive individual slot requests, or correction timeliness becomes a product requirement. A proposed trigger is deadline misses above 5% of eligible breaks or duplicate provider calls above 20%; tune these to actual cost and listening impact.

Move only discovery, normalization, evidence refresh and revision detection into a job first. It writes the same snapshots/story revisions. Selection remains per session at break preparation because it depends on what the listener heard and what record comes next. Use active-market demand and a short inactivity grace period if the “nothing runs when nobody listens” rule remains. Continuous alerts would be a separate, explicitly accepted product change.

Only then declare infrastructure in ../pof4-infra and follow that repository's plan/apply process. No infrastructure changes are needed for phases 0–3. The next concrete task is phase 0 plus the parser/cache/time repairs in phase 1—not a queue migration.

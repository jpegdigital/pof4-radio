Radio — the domain model

Decided 2026-09-04 in the slot-first design session; in the schema since 2026-09-04 (db/schema/*.sql is the source of truth, this page the reading of it). Replaced api.html, which described the segment world this one retires. The refactor that got us here is narrated in slot-first.md; how the pieces talk is sessions.html.

In one breath

A session is one listening: the ask and the voice. It owns slots, numbered from 1, each one clip over one track — the unit of the show and the only unit there is. A slot is born as a proposal (the proposer names a song and says why), grows its hits (what Qobuz found for that name), and is then written whole in one call: the pick (which hit), the chart (the writer's read of the track: ramp, post, outro, feel), the copy (the words) and the timing (two numbers). Then it is voiced: the clip. The track is the library's, not the session's: one row per record ever pulled into the bucket, carrying what Qobuz says about it and nothing anyone guessed. There is no playlist as a thing of its own — the playlist is the slots that have not been written yet. There are no segments: a break is a kind of slot, and the clock says which slots are breaks. Status is never stored; it is derived from what is present. Everything produced is kept forever.

session ─┬─ session_slot (seq 1)   ← always a break
         ├─ session_slot (seq 2)
         ├─ …
         └─ session_slot (seq N)   ← a break every breakEvery slots

track   ─ one row per record held in the bucket, keyed by Qobuz id; a slot points at it by its pick

1. The words

Radio's word where radio has one, Qobuz's where the thing is Qobuz's. Code uses these names and no synonyms: no record, song, card, candidate, playlist, segment, program.

2. session

Unchanged. Created instantly by POST /api/sessions; the ask, verbatim, and the voice. Doubles as the production lock: the fill rung and the slot rung take select … for update nowait on it, so at most one thing produces for a session at a time and a second producer gets a 409. The pull of a track is not under it — the track is the library's.

ColumnTypeWhat it is
iduuidPrimary key.

voice_idtextThe roster voice. The whole session is voiced in it.
created_at, updated_attimestamptzBookkeeping.

3. session_slot

One clip over one track. The whole show is this table, one row per position, and the row is written in phases by producers that never overlap in columns.

The life of a slot

PhaseWritten byLandsWhen
proposedthe fill rung: one Claude call (the proposer), then a Qobuz search per proposal in parallelthe proposal and its hitsat open, and whenever the unwritten slots run low
writtenthe slot rung: one Claude call (the writer)the pick, the chart, the copy, the timingone slot at a time, in order, just ahead of play
voicedthe slot rung, right after the write: ElevenLabsthe clipsame request; { again: true } re-voices only

A proposal with no streamable hit never becomes a row: the fill closes the gap, and asks the proposer for a couple more than it needs so a miss costs nothing. The pick is the first moment the track is known, so the track's pull is fired the moment the slot response lands and runs while the clip is voiced. While slot k plays, slot k+1 is written, pulled and voiced.

The row

create table session_slot (
  id            uuid primary key default gen_random_uuid(),
  session_id    uuid not null references session (id) on delete cascade,
  seq           integer not null,          -- 1-based position in the show

  -- the proposal, landed by the fill rung
  title         text not null,             -- the song as the proposer named it
  artist        text not null,
  why           text not null,             -- the proposer's line: why this song, here
  hits          jsonb not null,            -- [{id, title, artists, album, image, durationMs}] — the versions Qobuz found

  -- the pick, the chart, the copy and the timing: one Claude call, one update, immutable after
  qobuz_id      text,                      -- the pick: one of hits[].id
  ramp_ms       integer,                   -- chart: the instrumental run before the vocal; 0 if it opens on the vocal
  sure          boolean,                   -- chart: the writer is confident of ramp_ms to within a second or two
  post          text,                      -- chart: where the vocal lands, in words — never the lyric
  outro         text,                      -- chart: cold | fade
  outro_ms      integer,                   -- chart: how long the ending runs
  energy        integer,                   -- chart, feel: 1 (a ballad) to 5 (a floor-filler)
  tempo         text,                      -- chart, feel: down | mid | up
  mood          text,                      -- chart, feel: one line
  kind          text,                      -- copy: break | talkup | sweeper | segue
  words         text,                      -- copy: everything said; null for a segue
  lead_line     text,                      -- copy, breaks: the one sentence that talks the record in, said last
  legal_id      text,                      -- copy: said dry before the bed; the server's, when it is due
  treatment     text,                      -- copy: the writer's line: why this kind, here
  fallback      jsonb,                     -- {from, to, reason} when a house rule stepped the kind down
  record_under_ms integer,                 -- timing, breaks: how far before the voice ends the record starts under it
  voice_in_ms   integer,                   -- timing, talkups: how far into the record the voice comes in
  thinking      text,                      -- the writer's reasoning, a receipt; never on the wire

  -- the clip
  clip_key      text,                      -- bucket key; written only after the PUT succeeded
  voiced_at     timestamptz,               -- set ⇒ done, nothing more will ever happen to this slot

  created_at    timestamptz not null default now(),
  updated_at    timestamptz not null default now(),
  unique (session_id, seq)
);

Status is derived, never stored

StatusDerivationThe client can…
proposedrow exists, qobuz_id nullshow the title and artist as "coming up"; request the slot
writtenqobuz_id set, voiced_at nullpaint the tags from the pick, show the rundown's words, fire the pull
voicedvoiced_at setplay it, once the track's bytes are held

The pick's tags come from the hit it names, so the snapshot never joins track for words or art — only for whether the bytes are held (pulled, one where id = any(…) across the session). Readiness of the clip is as before: clip_key present always means the media exists; voiced_at without a key is a segue, done with nothing to say.

The kinds

The writer chooses the kind, except where the clock does: slot 1 is a break, and so is every slot the clock names. The house rules check the writer's timing against the writer's own chart and the house limits, stepping a kind down and recording why in fallback.

KindWhat you hearCopy needsTiming
breakVoice over a bed, the last line talks the record in under it. Legal ID first, dry, when it is due. The weather and the headlines are in the brief only here.words, lead_linerecord_under_ms
talkupThe record starts full; the voice comes in over the ramp and ends a beat before the post.wordsvoice_in_ms
sweeperA short dry ID, then a hard intro.words—
segueStraight out of the last record.nothing—

4. track

One row per record the bucket holds: the MP3 320 pulled from Qobuz once and kept forever, shared by every session that plays it. Keyed by the Qobuz id, the id a slot's pick carries. A row exists only after the PUT succeeded, so a row always means the bytes exist. The tags are copied from the picked hit at pull time, so the table reads as the library on its own.

ColumnTypeWhat it is
idtextThe Qobuz track id.
title, artists, album, image, duration_mstext, jsonb, text, text, integerThe tags, as Qobuz gave them. The art is Qobuz's CDN URL, not copied, until that breaks.
audio_keytexttracks/<id>.mp3 in the bucket.
bytesintegerThe MP3's size, for the bill.
created_attimestamptzBookkeeping.

Given and judged

The line between the two tables is not song versus show; it is given versus judged. What Qobuz hands us is given and lives on track, stable because nobody guessed it. What the writer decides — its read of the track as much as what it says over it — is judged and lives on the slot that judged it. Twelve words over a 14-second ramp and forty words over the same ramp are different charts of the same track, and both are right for their slot. Nothing from Claude ever lands on track. Earlier charts of the same track are offered to the writer in its brief as other DJs' notes, read-only; a session's charts never drift because a later session read the record differently. If a ramp is ever measured, that number is given, goes on track, and the brief says so.

5. The clock

Three settings rows shape every show; none is a client knob.

SettingWhat it doesDefault
breakEveryA break falls at slot 1 and every breakEvery slots after (1, 1 + k, 1 + 2k …). Between breaks the writer chooses.5
fillHow many slots a fill appends. The proposer is asked for a couple more to absorb misses.6
lowWaterWhen the count of proposed-but-unwritten slots drops to this, the client fires the next fill.2

7. What goes away

ThingWhy
session_segmentA segment was a break and its songs. The break is now a kind of slot and the clock places it; the playlist is the unwritten slots. Nothing is left for the segment to hold.
cardThe card was the chart made in a separate call and cached per track. The chart is now made in the same call as the copy, because the chart only ever serves the copy; a cross-check between two calls was the only thing the separate call bought. Prior slots are the cache.
The compose callThe proposer locks the order; there is nothing left to compose. The writer picks the version.
The program rung and the audio rungFolded into one slot rung: pick, chart, copy, timing, clip, one request.
station, segmentThe first build's, unused since 2026-09-03.

Radio — the shape

What the app is for and what flows where — the page that would still be true after every file was rewritten. It changes only when a box or an arrow changes. The API is sessions.html, the data domain.html; how the code should be cut is slices.md.

1. What it is for

A listener types an ask and gets a radio show made from their own Qobuz account: real recordings, a DJ who talks up the intros, a break with today's news and weather, the legal ID at the top of the hour. Three promises shape everything else:

  • It sounds like radio. Voice, bed and track are mixed to a plan — the DJ finishes where the vocal starts. A playback problem, solved in the browser.
  • First sound is fast and the show never runs dry. Production runs one slot ahead of the listener and no further. A sequencing problem, also the browser's.
  • Everything made is kept. Every judgment, script, take and track is retained and replayed as saved; nothing is regenerated to play. A storage discipline, the server's.

The shape that forces: the browser is the state machine; the server is stateless functions over one Postgres and one bucket. No queue, no worker pool, no session process — two small cron jobs are the only thing that runs without a listener.

Who does what among the minds: Claude has taste and words — it proposes the songs and writes the copy. Jev makes every judgment — which recording, what its chart is, how to mix it, which headlines. Code keeps the law — the clock, the budgets, what is valid.

Left to right: only the server and the cron workers touch the outside world, and only they write the store. The browser reads snapshots and bytes, and decides what to ask for next. Settings (the station's identity, the clock, the voice roster, the news desk) are rows read per request by every rung. Dev and prod share the one database and bucket on purpose.

3. The pipelines

Everything the app does is one of these five.

PipelineTriggerWhat happensLeaves behind
PrepareCron Read NWS and the RSS roster once; for news, Claude prepares and reviews facts from the evidence. A dated edition of news, of weather
FillBrowser: no slots yet, or the proposed ones are at low water Claude proposes the next songs with catalog lookups in hand; Qobuz search finds each one's versions; repeats dropped. Proposed slots: a proposal and its hits, nothing judged
SlotBrowser: the first unvoiced slot, one ahead of the cue Jev picks the recording, charts it and chooses the mix; on a break, Jev chooses from the prepared news; Claude writes; ElevenLabs voices. The slot's pick, plan, copy and receipts; the clip
TrackBrowser: the moment a pick is known Already in the library → held. Bytes in the bucket → the row rebuilt. Else pulled from Qobuz once. The track in the library, shared by every session after
PlaybackThe listener presses start The deck loads clip and track, lays the mix plan, runs mic, bed and track on one audio clock; a track ending advances the show. Sound; a receipt when a break's news was heard in full

Fill and Slot are rungs: idempotent, and serialized per session — a second producer is turned away. Track is not serialized, because a track belongs to the library, not the session.

Failure is staged, not atomic: a failed writer keeps the news reservation, a failed voice keeps the script, and the next request resumes from what is saved. Every stage's question, answer and probabilities are retained on the slot.

4. The laws

  • The browser decides, the server does. No server-side session state, queue or background work beyond the two cron jobs.
  • Rungs are idempotent and serialized per session. The track hold is neither locked nor session-owned.
  • Bucket first, row second. A row always means the bytes exist.
  • Status is derived from presence, never stored.
  • Staged failure. What was produced before a failure stays; the retry resumes, and never re-researches or re-selects.
  • Kept forever, played as saved. No regeneration and no freshness check at play time; another take is a new key.
  • No request-time research. A break reads the latest prepared edition and nothing else.
  • One call per outside service, no hidden retries or fallbacks. A judgment that fails stops the slot.
  • The clock is a setting, as are identity, voices and the news desk; a missing one is a fault, not a default.
  • The domain's words: track, tags, slot, proposal, hits, pick, chart, copy, timing, clip, fill, plan, the clock.
  • Few parts. One service, one database, one bucket; a dependency only when the platform and thirty lines won't do.
